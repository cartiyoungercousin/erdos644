\noindent\textbf{Purpose.} This brief is for one more research agent. It replaces the earlier brief
\texttt{OPEN\_QUESTION.md}, adding what six independent external reviews and our own latest work established. The goal is
to \emph{crack} the problem: prove $f(k,7)=(3/4+o(1))k$, or construct a counterexample.

Please do not send back reformulations; \S5 lists those that are already known. We want one of the three targets in
\S4 proved or refuted, or a construction.

Status tags: \st{VERIFIED} means a hand proof that passed an independent adversarial check; \st{EXACT} means exact
computation; \st{HAND} means a hand proof, unchecked; \st{HEUR} means heuristic.

\section{The problem}
Let $H$ be a family of $k$-sets on $V$, $|V|=n$, with transversal number $\tau$. $H$ has property $(7,2)$ if every at most 7
members have a transversal of size at most 2. A \emph{good seven} is 7 members (repeats allowed) with no 2-point
transversal. Put $f(k,7)=\max\tau$ over $k$-uniform $(7,2)$ families.
\begin{itemize}
\item Known: $f(k,7)\le\lceil7k/8\rceil$ and $f(4m,7)\ge3m+1$ for $m\ge10$ (Fon-Der-Flaass, Kostochka, Woodall 1999).
\item Erd\H{o}s asked whether $f(k,7)=(3/4+o(1))k$.
\item Working exact conjecture: $f(k,7)=\lfloor(3k+5)/4\rfloor$ for $k\ge9$.
\item No $(7,2)$ family with $\tau-3k/4>5/4$ is known \st{EXACT data, $k\le20$}.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Co-edge form} (used throughout). Put $T=\tau-1$, $r=n-k-T$ (the covering radius), $s=n-7k/4$ and
$e=\tau-3k/4=s-r+1$ (the excess). The co-edges $\mathcal C=\{V\setminus B\}$ have size $n-k=T+r$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\tau\ge T+1$ $\iff$ every $T$-set lies in a co-edge ($\mathcal C$ is \emph{$T$-covering}).
\item Seven members form a good seven $\iff$ their seven co-edges cover all pairs of $V$.
\item A \emph{Fano certificate} is seven co-edges $K_1,\dots,K_7$ indexed by the points of the Fano plane with
$K_a\cup K_b\cup K_c=V$ for every line $abc$. A Fano certificate is a good seven, at every $n$.
\item \st{VERIFIED, exact LP}: for $n<11k/6$ every good seven is a Fano certificate.
\item \st{EXACT data}: for $n\ge2k$ there are families at record $\tau$ whose only good sevens are non-Fano (``Fano minus a
line'' types).
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Pencils.} A \emph{pencil} is three members with empty common intersection. Its weight is
$|x_1\triangle x_2\triangle x_3|$.

\section{The wall (what everything runs into)}
Six independent external reviews and about a dozen internal attacks all stop at the \emph{same} missing statement:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Covering must be turned into a coordinated choice of seven members, or else a stability alternative must land the
family in a closed region} (a light pencil, a near-complete block, a rich family, or a coset layer).
\end{quote}
The pieces of this are established:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Pencils are not enough} \st{HEUR, exact binomial margins}. Take random unions of about $n^3$ cosets of a random
linear code at covering codimension. They are covering, thin and block-free, far from every coset, and have every triple
sum heavy. Yet they have abundant good sevens. So every argument that only forces light pencils stops near
$e\le0.18\,s$.
\item \textbf{Static finishes are not enough} (Theorem O, \st{HAND + exact certificate}). For $\nu=n/k>2+t$ an explicit
two-cluster adversary defeats every bounded combination of covering lemmas with every static ``light configuration
$\Rightarrow$ seven'' lemma (P1, HUB and all 715 seven classes).
\begin{itemize}
\item Its pencils have profile $\{ck,o(k),o(k)\}$ with $c\in(1/2,9/16]$: two members share $ck$ points, and the third meets each in $o(k)$ ``glue'' points.
\item Consequence: these tools cannot beat $7/8$ for $\nu\ge23/8$.
\item What is needed: \emph{adaptive} finishing, where later requests depend on where earlier replies actually landed.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Structure theorems fail at this density} \st{HAND}. The Fano pattern has true complexity 2, so any natural
obstruction is quadratic. Full quadric level sets nevertheless acquire sevens once $4n-7k\ge128$. There is no pseudorandom
majorant for transference. Coset-based inverse statements are vacuous or false.
\item \textbf{Random colourings are not enough} \st{HAND}. A random Fano colouring with the union bound gives only slope
$3/7$, and bounds that use only marginals cannot beat it.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Three precise targets}

\subsection*{Target 1 (core, $n<11k/6$ and beyond): the Fano-repair dichotomy}
An external review supplied an adaptive finishing criterion, which we have checked for plausibility.
\begin{thm}[weighted Fano repair \st{HAND}]
Fix a pool $Y\subseteq V$ with $|Y|=c-T$ ($c=n-k$), and put $X=V\setminus Y$, $m=|X|=k+T$. Play 7 adaptive request
rounds, indexed by the Fano points in some order. Let the reply in round $i$ be a co-edge $K_i$ with omission set
$M_i=Y\setminus K_i$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Hypothesis.} Caps $\gamma_{ab}$ are known in advance, valid for every history, with
$|M_a\cap M_b|\le\gamma_{ab}$. For each line $\lambda$ let $(a_\lambda,b_\lambda)$ be its two earlier points.
\item \emph{Conclusion.} If $Q=\sum_\lambda\gamma_{a_\lambda b_\lambda}\le4T-3k$ (first order; an exact integral version
exists), the requester obtains a Fano certificate.
\item \emph{Strategy.} Round $i$ requests its share of $X$ together with $\bigcup_{\lambda:\,i_\lambda=i}(M_{a_\lambda}\cap
M_{b_\lambda})$.
\item \emph{Corollaries.} A random order gives $Q\le\frac13\sum_{a<b}\gamma_{ab}$. Uniform caps $\eta\le4\varepsilon k/7$
recover the known near-pool repair.
\end{itemize}
\end{thm}
\begin{tgt}[1]
For every $\varepsilon>0$ and large $k$, every $T$-covering co-edge family with $T\ge(3/4+\varepsilon)k$ satisfies one of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item it admits a pool $Y$ and an adaptive request scheme whose pairwise omission caps satisfy the budget above;
\item it contains a pencil of weight $\le4T-3k-6$ (then P1 finishes);
\item it contains a near-complete block covered by NC or NC-P;
\item it is rich (then RA finishes);
\item it contains a coset layer covered by Theorems D and $9'$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tgt}
\noindent\emph{Why this is the right target.} It is genuinely adaptive, so Theorem O does not apply. Every branch ends in
a proved finish. The hard case is large aggregate overlap that is \emph{not} a single common core; the earlier attempt to
infer a common core from the overlap was refuted. \emph{Litmus test:} any proof must find sevens in the random coset
unions of \S3.1, which have no light pencil, no block and no coset structure. So in those families branch (i) must
fire.

\subsection*{Target 2 (global, all $n$): the multi-row shadow-transfer lemma}
\begin{thm}[private shadows \st{HAND}, from an external review; checked by us]
Let $H$ be a counterexample of minimum total size $\sum|B|$ among families of nonempty sets of size $\le k$ with $(7,2)$
and $\tau>T$ (any $n$). Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\tau=T+1$;
\item every member $B$ has a private $T$-set $S_B$ disjoint from $B$ that hits all other members;
\item for every $z\in B$, the members avoiding $z$ have $S_B$ as a minimum transversal, with witnesses $C_{z,s}$ satisfying
$C_{z,s}\cap S_B=\{s\}$;
\item the shadows $E_{z,s}=C_{z,s}\setminus S_B$, for at least $T-2$ columns $s$, form a $(7,2)$ family of nonempty sets of
size $\le k-1$.
\end{itemize}
\end{thm}
\begin{tgt}[2]
Using all $k$ rows $z\in B$ together (they share the columns $S_B$ and the trace condition), show that small transversals
of the row-shadow families combine into a transversal of $H$ of size $\le T$, or force a good seven among the lifted
$C_{z,s}$.
\end{tgt}
\noindent\emph{Why this matters.} It is induction on uniformity in the unconfined problem. It sidesteps the Fano window,
lifting and large $n$ simultaneously. A single row has no lower bound on its $\tau$, so the coupling across rows is the
entire content.

\subsection*{Target 3 (large $n$): adaptive play against Theorem O}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Question.} Exhibit an adaptive finishing strategy, of bounded depth with request sets depending on earlier
replies, that defeats the two-cluster glue adversary of \S3.2. Better: show that, combined with P1, HUB and the pencil
bound, it gives $e\le c\,k$ with $c<1/8$ for all $n$. That would be the first bound below $7k/8$.
\item \textbf{A lead.} An unrefereed external manuscript (\emph{six-sevenths}) claims $f(k,7)\le6k/7+O(1)$ by adaptive
request arguments. Its Lemmas 4.1--4.8 are machine-checked in Lean by us. Its Lemmas 2.5, 2.6 and Proposition 5.1, and the
final assembly, are not yet checked. An internal test of adaptive play against Theorem O is running.
\item \textbf{The non-Fano side ($n\ge2k$).} The sevens that occur are ``Fano minus a line''. Their analogue of P1 (the HUB
lemma, \st{HAND}) is: a pencil with
$\sum_i\max(0,d_{ij}+d_{il}-d_{jl}-(k-T))\le4T-3k$ gives a good seven of the relevant class at every $n$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Already known; please do not resend}
\begin{itemize}
\item Reformulations: the complement/co-edge form; good seven $=$ pair cover by 7 co-edges; the six-member kernel (six
fixed members are completed by a member avoiding the union $D(F)$ of their 1- and 2-point transversals); the active graph
of 2-point transversals; the ordered four-query Fano construction; completion-fibre counting.
\item The fractional pair-cover LP has value $\le\binom n2/\binom T2<6$ \st{known}. It does not round to an integral seven:
over-pooled families have integral cover number $\ge8$ at the threshold.
\item Fano-incidence stability: any Fano certificate has $\le4s$ exceptional points and near-balanced line classes.
\item Dead routes, each for a recorded reason:
\begin{itemize}
\item pencil-only methods (\S3.1);
\item static finishes (\S3.2);
\item random colourings with union or Janson bounds;
\item coarse-threshold structure (false);
\item coset or quadratic inverse theorems and transference (\S3.3);
\item slice rank, Chevalley--Warning and MacWilliams;
\item the topological rainbow criterion (connectivity out of reach);
\item compression to $n\le2k$ via $\tau$-critical subfamilies;
\item containers, regularity, entropy and dependent random choice, junta and spread lemmas;
\item fractional relaxation with rounding, flag algebras, bounded-depth games aimed at exactly $3/4$, linear gluing, and
density increment at density $e^{-\gamma k}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Test families (check every idea against these)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Complete:} all $k$-subsets of $[n]$, with $\tau=n-k+1$. For example $K^{(4m)}_{7m-1}$ is $(7,2)$, which
attains the one-anchor bound. Complete families attain $\lfloor(3k+5)/4\rfloor$ for $k\not\equiv0\pmod4$ \st{derived}.
\item \textbf{FKW parity ($k=4m$):} the record families, with excess 1 and $r=1$. Example: $k=12$, $|V|=22$, $X\subset V$ with
$|X|=12$, $H=\{A:|A\cap X|\ \text{odd}\}$, $\tau=10$. It is $(7,2)$ by exhaustive computation.
\item \textbf{Random coset unions:} the pencil barrier of \S3.1. They are covering and have sevens, but no light pencils.
\item \textbf{Two-cluster glue adversary:} the static-finish barrier of \S3.2.
\item \textbf{Non-Fano-only profile families} ($n\ge2k$): e.g.\ $k=10$, cells of sizes $(10,13)$,
$J=\{(1,9),(7,3)\}$, $\tau=9$.
\end{itemize}

\section{What we are asking for}
One of the following, with status tags and exact checks of any construction ($\tau$ by ILP, seven-freeness by an
independent exhaustive or SAT check):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item a proof or refutation of Target 1, 2 or 3, or of a precise special case that the litmus tests show is non-trivial;
\item a covering family with $\tau-3k/4$ growing (or merely $>5/4$ at some $k$) and no good seven;
\item a reason why Targets 1--3 are all out of reach of known methods, naming the mechanism that is missing.
\end{enumerate}
