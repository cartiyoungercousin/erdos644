\begin{abstract}
Erd\H{o}s asked whether $f(k,7)=(\tfrac34+o(1))k$, where $f(k,7)$ is the largest transversal number of a
$k$-uniform family in which every at most seven members can be pierced by two points. The best published bounds are
$f(k,7)\le\lceil 7k/8\rceil$ and $f(4m,7)\ge 3m+1$ for $m\ge 10$ (Fon-Der-Flaass, Kostochka and Woodall, 1999).
This report records what the project has established and how each claim has been checked.
\textbf{The problem is not solved}, and no new bound on $f(k,7)$ is claimed. The established results are: a verified
reduction of the problem to a covering game on at most $3k$ points; proofs closing all but one region of that game,
together with two small residues; and theorems excluding broad classes of counterexamples in the remaining region.
The open problem is stated in a precise, self-contained form.
\end{abstract}

\section{Verification standard}
Each claim carries one of the following levels.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lvl{LEAN}: machine-checked in Lean 4/Mathlib, using only the standard axioms and no \texttt{sorry}.
\item \lvl{VERIFIED}: a hand proof checked by an independent reviewer. The reviewer was given only the statement and
the proof, checked every step, and ran its own exact computations. These included adversarial instances built
against the weakest step.
\item \lvl{EXACT}: exact rational or brute-force computation on finite instances.
\end{itemize}
Claims below these levels (unverified hand proofs, floating-point evidence) are listed separately in the claim ledger
\texttt{notes/LEDGER.md} and are not used here.

\section{Framework}
\textbf{Request game.} If $\tau(H)>T$, every set $S$ of $T$ points misses some edge of $H$. A requester names $T$-sets;
an adversary answers each with an edge avoiding it. The requester wins if seven answers have no 2-point transversal.
If she asks every $T$-set, the answers along that single play form a family with transversal number above $T$.
Hence she wins against every adversary if and only if $f(k,7)\le T$. With boundedly many requests, only requester wins
are conclusive.

\textbf{Confinement} \lvl{VERIFIED}. Three answers with empty common intersection confine every 2-point transversal to
their union $V$, with $|V|\le 3k$, when the three anchors are kept among the final seven. Requester wins of that form
transfer to the full problem. \emph{Caveat:} the closed-region results below are stated for families confined to $V$
and do not keep the anchors. Transferring them needs anchor-keeping versions or a far-replies lemma (gap G2), which is
open.

\textbf{Co-edge form.} Work in units of $k$: $t=T/k=\tfrac34+\varepsilon$ and $N=|V|/k$. The co-edges are
$K=V\setminus B$, each of mass $N-1$. The hypothesis $\tau>T$ becomes \emph{$T$-covering}: every $T$-subset of $V$ lies
in a co-edge. A winning seven is seven co-edges covering every pair of $V$. It suffices to prove everything for all
sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.

\subsection{Tools}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Finishing configurations} \lvl{VERIFIED}: the slack count, Theorem~T (a triangle of co-edges finished by
four requests) and Theorem~Q (a quadrangle finish).
\item \textbf{Reduction} \lvl{VERIFIED}: the core-window statement follows from $(\Sigma)$, the existence of one of
three explicit configurations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Exclusion of structured counterexamples in the core window} \lvl{VERIFIED}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Theorem S}: no family whose membership depends only on profiles of a bounded partition is a
counterexample.
\item \textbf{Theorem S$^+$}: the same at finite $k$, for up to $4\varepsilon k-12$ cells of arbitrary sizes.
\item \textbf{Ordered rules}: rules with fixed in-cell orders are not counterexamples.
\item \textbf{Theorem W}: greedy priority-order adversaries whose choice of order depends on the request only through a
window of at most $T-k/2$ points lose within seven requests, whatever the number of orders.
\item \textbf{Theorem H}: if more than $2/3$ of the lines over one kernel are present, a winning seven exists. The
constant $2/3$ is sharp.
\item \textbf{Theorem R}: uniformly random or 6-wise independent selectors lose unless they use about $e^{1.19k}$
classes (second-moment argument).
\end{itemize}
Taken together, a counterexample to $\tfrac34$ must be deterministic, must use more than about $k/4$ points of
information about each request, and must be strongly anti-correlated. Random families of the relevant size contain
$e^{\Theta(k)}$ winning sevens.

\subsection{Machine-checked lemmas} \lvl{LEAN}
About twenty lemmas are formalised:
\begin{itemize}
\item the confinement and petal reductions;
\item the Fano criterion and Fano blocks;
\item the flat 7-tuple lemma, the waste adversary and several transversal bounds;
\item Lemmas 4.1--4.8 of an unrefereed $6/7$ manuscript.
\end{itemize}
The $6/7$ theorem itself is not formalised, and we do not endorse it.

\subsection{Small cases} \lvl{EXACT}
Exact certificates show that every $6$- and $7$-uniform family with $\tau=k$ contains seven members with no 2-cover.
Combined with the confinement reduction, this gives $f(6,7)=5$ and $f(7,7)=6$. The matching lower bounds depend on
covering numbers that are still being checked against published tables, so these two values are provisional.

\section{Methods shown not to apply in the core window}
Each of the following was analysed and shown to fail for an identified structural reason.
\begin{itemize}
\item Junta approximation: the families are exponentially sparse.
\item Spread and sunflower lemmas: the families are at most $2.3$-spread.
\item Fractional relaxation followed by rounding: the fractional transversal number is $O(k^{1/3})$.
\item Hypergraph containers: the codegree conditions fail.
\item Flag algebras: the positivity constraints are automatic.
\item Density-only and forbidden-pattern arguments.
\item Adaptive restriction to links: links move the parameters away from every closed region.
\item Entropy methods: the certificates need cyclic constraints, and entropy controls only acyclic ones.
\item Covering-design stability.
\end{itemize}
These analyses are hand proofs. Some are still awaiting independent verification; see the ledger. They identify what a
successful argument must use: the full covering hypothesis, not only density, together with adaptive choice.

\section{Open problems}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Core window.} For small $\varepsilon$ and $5t-2<N<3-t$, show that every $T$-covering co-edge family
contains seven members covering all pairs. Equivalently, by the verified reduction, show that it contains one of the
configurations $(\Sigma)$. (By the verified reduction, $(\Sigma)$ implies the core-window statement.)
\item \textbf{Lifting.} Transfer the confined results to general families, either by anchor-keeping certificates or by
showing that far replies cannot help the adversary (gap G2).
\item \textbf{Petal residue A.} A pooled two-arena adversary for $\tfrac34<t<\tfrac79$, in the range
$\tfrac t2-\tfrac16\le\delta<1-t$. In a restricted model this adversary provably beats all known finishes.
\item \textbf{Petal residue B.} Two-class families not of arena form. This has been reduced to a one-class stability
statement, and exact search at $k=12,16$ found no example.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Assessment}
The project has not changed the known bounds on $f(k,7)$. What it has done is the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For families confined to at most $3k$ points, it reduced the asymptotic question to a single explicit statement
about covering families on $1.75k$--$2.25k$ points, plus two small residues. A separate lifting step to general
families remains open.
\item It proved, with independent verification, that every structured or random-like candidate counterexample fails.
\item It identified precisely why the standard tools of extremal set theory do not reach the remaining case.
\end{enumerate}
In our assessment this is substantial progress in understanding the problem. The decisive step, a proof or a
construction for anti-correlated families in the core window, has not been achieved.
